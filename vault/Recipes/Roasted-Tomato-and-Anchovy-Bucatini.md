# Roasted Tomato and Anchovy Bucatini

Anchovies melted into slow roasted tomatoes with soft, barely caramelized red onion. Tossed with bucatini and buried in Parmesan.

Canned tomatoes work. Olive oil roasted tomatoes are the real version. Roasting drives off water, caramelizes the sugars and concentrates everything.

## Ingredients

### Sauce

- 1/4 cup olive oil, plus more for drizzling
- 1/2 small red onion, very thinly sliced
- Kosher salt
- Crushed red pepper flakes
- 4 anchovy fillets
- 2 tbsp tomato paste
- 1 recipe Olive Oil Roasted Tomatoes, or 1 (28 oz) can whole peeled tomatoes, crushed

### Pasta

- 12 oz bucatini or spaghetti
- Lots of grated Parmesan

## Instructions

### 1. Start the water

Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil.

### 2. Cook the onion slowly

Heat the olive oil in a large skillet or heavy bottomed pan over medium. Add the onion. Season with salt and a pinch of red pepper flakes. Cook, stirring now and then, 10 to 15 minutes until totally soft but not browned.

### 3. Melt the anchovies and toast the paste

Add the anchovies. Stir about 30 seconds until they dissolve into the pan. Add the tomato paste. Cook about 90 seconds until it turns brick red and starts to stick to the bottom.

### 4. Add the tomatoes

Add the roasted or canned tomatoes. Stir and simmer while the pasta cooks so the sauce thickens a little. Taste for salt.

### 5. Cook the pasta

Cook the pasta in the boiling water. Drain, reserving 1 cup of the pasta water.

### 6. Toss

Add the pasta and 1/2 cup of the pasta water to the skillet. Toss to coat. Keep cooking and tossing until the sauce clings to every noodle. Add more pasta water a splash at a time if it tightens up.

### 7. Serve

Pull off the heat. Move to one large bowl or four smaller ones. Top with lots of Parmesan and a drizzle of olive oil.

## Notes

### Do ahead

The tomato sauce keeps 5 days in the fridge or 1 month frozen. Make a batch in summer when tomatoes are cheap and good. Freeze it for winter.

### Flavor map

- Anchovies dissolve completely. No fishy taste, just deep savory salt. This is the backbone of the sauce.
- Slow cooked red onion is sweet and soft. It should never brown.
- Tomato paste toasted until brick red adds concentrated depth without extra liquid.
- Roasted tomatoes bring caramelized sweetness that canned cannot match.
- Parmesan finishes it with salt and fat. Do not be shy.

### Kitchen reminders

- 10 to 15 minutes on the onion is not a typo. Low and slow. If it starts to color, turn the heat down.
- Bucatini is thick spaghetti with a hole through the middle. Spaghetti is the swap.
- Reserve the pasta water before you drain. Easy to forget.
