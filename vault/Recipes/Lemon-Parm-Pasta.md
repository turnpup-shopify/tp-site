# Lemon Parm Pasta with Capers and Calabrian Chile

Glossy lemon and Parmigiano sauce built in the pan. Capers bring the salt, Calabrian chile brings the depth and heat. Brighter and punchier than the classic version.

Use what you have. This sauce is flexible.

## Ingredients

### Pasta

- 1 lb pasta
- 3/4 to 1 cup reserved pasta water

### Cheese paste

- 1 1/4 cups finely grated Parmigiano-Reggiano
- 4 tbsp fresh lemon juice
- 2 tsp lemon zest
- 2 tbsp extra virgin olive oil

### Butter base

- 4 tbsp butter
- 1 1/2 tbsp capers, drained and roughly chopped
- 1 to 2 tsp Calabrian chile paste (start small, it is potent)
- 1/2 tsp black pepper
- Salt, go light at first since capers and cheese are salty

### To finish

- Optional parsley or basil

## Instructions

### 1. Cook the pasta

Boil the pasta in salted water until just shy of al dente. Scoop out 1 cup of pasta water before draining. Drain.

### 2. Make the cheese paste

In a bowl mix the Parmigiano, lemon juice, lemon zest and olive oil into a thick paste. Set aside.

### 3. Bloom the capers and chile

Melt the butter gently in a large pan over low heat. Add the capers and Calabrian chile paste. Warm them 30 to 60 seconds to bloom the flavor. Do not brown the butter.

### 4. Toss the pasta

Add the drained pasta to the pan. Toss to coat in the butter.

### 5. Add the cheese off heat

Pull the pan off the heat. Add the cheese paste and toss.

### 6. Loosen with pasta water

Add pasta water a splash at a time, tossing constantly, until the sauce is glossy and emulsified and clings to the pasta. Finish with black pepper. Taste before adding salt.

Top with parsley or basil if using. Serve right away.

## Notes

### Swaps that worked

- No pecorino. The classic version uses pecorino plus Parmigiano. All Parmigiano is milder, so the sauce comes out softer and rounder.
- Capers stand in for pecorino's sharp salt.
- Calabrian chile adds depth and heat without stepping on the lemon.

### Flavor map

- Parmigiano is nutty and savory. Milder and rounder than pecorino.
- Lemon juice is the acid. Zest carries the aroma. Both matter.
- Capers are briny and sharp. They do the job pecorino would have done.
- Calabrian chile is fruity heat with a little smoke. Start at 1 tsp.

### Kitchen reminders

- Cheese goes in off heat or it clumps and turns grainy.
- Starchy pasta water is what makes the sauce emulsify. Do not skip reserving it.
- Grate the cheese fine. Coarse shreds melt unevenly.
