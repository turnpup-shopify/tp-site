
## 1. What the research says (and why most practice fails)

- **Quantity breeds quality.** In a 246-person study, people who produced more ideas also produced more creative ones (r = 0.73, judged by independent raters). Your first five ideas are the obvious ones everyone has; the value is in ideas 6–20.
- **Rote repetition makes you _less_ novel.** Stanford (Brucks & Huang, 2020) had 413 people practice divergent thinking daily for 12 days. Novelty went _down_. The brain grooves a path and keeps walking it. Fix: change the prompt, the constraint, and the lens every session. Rotate frameworks; never settle on a favourite.
- **Mornings win.** The same study found habitual thinking takes over late in the day. Generate early, judge later.
- **Separate generating from judging.** Never in the same pass. Judging while generating kills the weird ideas before they can mutate into good ones.
- **Feed the machine.** James Webb Young's _A Technique for Producing Ideas_ (1940): an idea is a new combination of old elements. If you only consume your own field, you can only recombine your own field.

## 2. Generating frameworks (pick ONE per session, rotate)

|Framework|The move|Best when|
|---|---|---|
|**Altucher's 10 ideas**|Write 10 ideas on one topic, no judging. Stuck? Make it 20 — the pressure is the problem.|Daily base workout|
|**SCAMPER**|Run the thing through 7 lenses: Substitute, Combine, Adapt, Modify/Magnify, Put to other use, Eliminate, Reverse.|Improving something that exists|
|**First principles**|Strip to undeniable truths ("what is this _for_?"), then rebuild without inherited assumptions. Dyson: a vacuum separates dust from air — why a bag?|You suspect the whole category is wrong|
|**Inversion** (Munger)|Ask "how would I guarantee this fails?" then reverse each answer.|Anything you're stuck on; finds hidden constraints|
|**How Might We**|Reframe the problem 5 different ways before solving. Too broad = useless; too narrow = embedded solution.|You're anchored on one solution|
|**Forced constraints**|"What if it had to be free? Ship in 2 days? One feature? For a 7-year-old? Cost 10×?"|Ideas feel incremental; also proven to keep novelty up over time|
|**Analogous worlds**|How does nature / Formula 1 / a hospital / a monastery solve this? Steal the mechanism, not the surface.|You need something that doesn't look like the competition|
|**Combinatorial play**|Pick two random nouns and force a connection to your problem.|Pure muscle-building, loosens habitual paths|
|**Worst possible idea**|Generate the 10 worst ideas, then ask what's secretly good in each.|Group is tense or everyone is being "sensible"|
|**Name the Unnamed**|Find a behaviour your audience already does but has no word for. Label it (medical / legal register works). Confess it as your own flaw. Prove it with a fast montage. Ask "is it just me?"|Content that needs to be shared, not just watched. Recognition beats information.|

## 3. Sharpening frameworks (the judging pass — a separate session)

- **The 3 filters:** Is it _novel_ (not just unique)? Is it _useful_ (who wants this, badly)? Is it _feasible_ (what's the next step)? Altucher: an idea is too big if you can't name the next step.
- **Pre-mortem:** It's 12 months later and it flopped. Write the obituary. Fix what you can; drop what you can't.
- **Steelman the opposite:** Argue the strongest case that the idea is wrong. If you can't, you don't understand it yet.
- **Idea sex:** Take your top two ideas and combine them. The hybrid is often better than either.
- **Six Thinking Hats** (de Bono): walk the idea through facts, feelings, risks, benefits, creativity, process — one at a time, so no single mode dominates.

## 4. The training process

**Daily (10 min, morning):**

1. Pick a topic (rotate: work, product, personal, absurd).
2. Pick a _different_ framework than yesterday.
3. Write 10 ideas. Numbers 1–5 will be obvious. Don't stop. Numbers 6–10 are the workout.
4. Star one. Write its next step in one line. Close the notebook.

**Weekly (20 min):**

- Judging pass on the week's starred ideas using the 3 filters + a pre-mortem on the best one.
- Idea sex: combine two starred ideas.

**Always:**

- **Reverse-engineer one piece of great creative a day.** Not "how do I copy it" but "what is the reusable mechanism and what are its inputs?" Write it as a 5-step recipe. 30 days = 30 frameworks sourced from what works now. (Name the Unnamed came from one reel.)
- Stack lenses when one feels stale. The grillodesigns reel = Analogous Worlds (medical register) + Inversion (confess the flaw instead of flexing) + Forced Constraint (no product until the end). The combination is the novelty.
- Carry a capture tool; ideas arrive when you're not trying (Young's "incubation" stage is real).
- Read outside your field 20 min/day. Raw material.
- Track the metric that matters: _how fast you get to idea #6_, not how good idea #1 is.

**Expected timeline:** Altucher claims the "idea machine" switch flips at roughly 6 months of daily practice; the muscle atrophies after ~2 weeks off.
